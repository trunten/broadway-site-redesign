Why study Music?

Music develops performance, composition, listening, creativity and collaboration while introducing students to music from different cultures and traditions.

Curriculum at Broadway

The curriculum is practical at its core. Students learn through performing, composing, listening and reflection, supported by links with Birmingham music provision and co-curricular ensembles and activities.

Year 7

Six projects introduce the subject through performance, listening, composition and reflection. Work includes Peter and the Wolf and orchestral instruments, keyboard composition and graphic notation, vocal music from different cultures, body percussion and polyrhythm, Hall of the Mountain King and an introduction to ukulele.

Year 8

Students build on Year 7 through more complex performance and composition. Projects broaden the range of styles, including Blues, and deepen rhythmic, melodic and ensemble skills.

Years 9–11

Students who continue the subject develop more independent performance, composition and listening skills, using musical vocabulary and stylistic understanding in preparation for qualification work.

Beyond lessons

The department supports choirs, bands, instrumental work and other activities, giving students opportunities to rehearse and perform with others.

Assessment and progression

Students are assessed through practical performance and composition as well as listening, reflection and use of musical vocabulary. Increasing independence and accuracy are expected as students progress.

Where this can lead

Music can lead to performance, composition, production, sound engineering, arts management, teaching, media and the wider creative industries.
