Why study Design Technology?

Design Technology is a practical and creative subject in which students design and make solutions to real problems, drawing on mathematics, science, engineering, computing and art.

Curriculum at Broadway

Students learn to be resourceful designers and makers: investigating needs, generating ideas, working safely with materials and processes, testing prototypes and evaluating the impact of design on people and the wider world.

Year 7

A rotation system introduces resistant materials, textiles and food, with practical projects supported by the theory behind materials, processes and safe making.

Years 8–9

Rotations broaden into textiles and food, STEM and graphics, and resistant materials. Skills become more precise and students take increasing responsibility for design decisions.

Years 10–11

GCSE Design Technology covers core and specialist technical principles plus designing and making. Year 10 develops theory and practical design experience; Year 11 centres on the AQA contextual challenge and substantial design-and-make project.

Assessment and progression

AQA GCSE Design Technology is split equally between the written examination and non-exam assessment. The NEA is a substantial iterative design-and-make project supported by a portfolio of evidence.

Where this can lead

Progression includes engineering, construction, product and industrial design, architecture, graphics, textiles and fashion, product development, retail and manufacturing.
