Why study Geography?

Geography helps students understand the interaction between people, places and the natural world, with a strong focus on contemporary global issues.

Curriculum at Broadway

The curriculum is designed to create “21st-century geographers”: knowledgeable about places and processes, confident with cartographic, graphical and statistical skills, and able to make connections between human and physical geography.

Year 7

Creating a Sustainable World, Ecosystem Interactions, Russia, Urban Interactions, Weather Processes and Geographical Investigations. Students begin to learn like geographers and develop fieldwork and data skills.

Year 8

Changing Landscapes, Conflict & Peace, the Middle East, Glacial Processes, Changing Coastlines and Fighting Inequality deepen understanding of how human and natural systems interact.

Year 9

Africa, Changing Hazards and Great British Geography move students towards GCSE-level thinking and increasingly evaluative conclusions.

Sixth Form

AQA A Level Geography includes Water and Carbon Cycles, Hazards and Changing Places, plus an independent fieldwork investigation and advanced synoptic geographical thinking.

Assessment and progression

Regular retrieval, testing, extended writing, fieldwork and data interpretation develop the knowledge and skills needed for GCSE and A Level. Students are expected to use evidence to explain, compare and evaluate geographical issues.

Where this can lead

Geography is valued in routes including environmental science, engineering, law, finance, politics, GIS, urban planning, geology, ecology, conservation, logistics and international development.
