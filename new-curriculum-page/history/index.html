Why study History?

History helps students think critically about evidence, understand how interpretations are formed and build a rounded picture of change, conflict and society over time.

Curriculum at Broadway

Students learn to “think like a historian”: asking questions of sources, weighing evidence, constructing arguments and studying different periods and parts of the world rather than seeing the past as a single story.

Year 7

Students establish core chronological and disciplinary skills and learn how historians use evidence and construct interpretations, creating the foundations for later thematic and period studies.

Year 8

The Industrial Revolution and its impact; the British Empire, including India and historians’ interpretations; and the causes, events and consequences of the First World War.

Year 9

The Second World War and Holocaust, followed by post-war Britain including the NHS, welfare state, immigration and changing roles, alongside wider global change including the partition of India.

GCSE

AQA studies include Britain: Power and the People; Germany 1890–1945; Conflict and Tension 1918–39; and Elizabethan England.

Assessment and progression

Students are assessed through knowledge recall, source analysis, interpretations and increasingly extended historical arguments. GCSE is assessed through two written examinations.

Where this can lead

History supports careers and study in law, politics, journalism, heritage, museums, archives, education, research, the Civil Service and many roles that depend on evidence and argument.
