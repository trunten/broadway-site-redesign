Science

Why study Science?

Science develops curiosity about the natural world and the knowledge and evidence-based reasoning needed to understand biology, chemistry and physics.

Curriculum at Broadway

Broadway uses a spiral curriculum organised around big scientific ideas, so important knowledge is revisited in greater depth. Practical work is used to develop accurate methods, recording, analysis and scientific judgement as well as factual understanding.

Key Stage 3

Ten big ideas — Forces, Electromagnetism, Energy, Waves, Matter, Reactions, Earth, Organisms, Ecosystems and Genes — provide the structure. Students revisit these ideas with increasing complexity.

Year 7 example

Students develop particle and separation ideas in chemistry, cells and interdependence in biology, and practical scientific skills. Ecosystems and interdependence are connected to first-hand learning at Island Pool.

Years 8–9

The spiral design extends earlier ideas into more demanding chemistry, biology and physics, including global issues such as climate, Earth resources and human impact.

Key Stage 4

GCSE science develops the same big ideas through deeper theory and required practical work, preparing students to apply knowledge, analyse evidence and solve unfamiliar scientific problems.

Sixth Form

Post-16 science builds subject-specific depth and independence for students progressing into science, medicine, engineering and related routes.

Assessment and progression

Knowledge recall, application, mathematical and data skills, required practical work and extended scientific explanations are assessed throughout. Students are expected to plan methods, record results and draw evidence-based conclusions.

Where this can lead

Science supports routes into medicine, healthcare, pharmacy, engineering, environmental science, research, forensic science, technology and many other STEM careers.
