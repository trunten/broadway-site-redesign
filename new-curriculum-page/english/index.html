Why study English?

English develops confident readers, writers and speakers, combining a love of literature with the communication skills students need throughout life.

Curriculum at Broadway

Language and Literature are deliberately interwoven. Students encounter challenging literary and non-fiction texts, learn how writers create meaning, and practise writing accurately and purposefully for different audiences and forms.

Year 7

Students meet a range of enjoyable and challenging literary texts, exploring ideas, themes, characters and settings alongside the craft of the writer. The curriculum also integrates topical non-fiction and real-world texts linked to the literature being studied.

Years 8–9

Reading becomes increasingly analytical and comparative. Students encounter a wider range of prose, drama and poetry, while developing narrative, descriptive, argumentative and transactional writing and increasingly precise spoken communication.

Years 10–11

GCSE English Language and English Literature bring together the reading and writing developed at Key Stage 3. Students study Shakespeare, a nineteenth-century novel, modern drama and poetry while preparing for unseen and non-fiction reading and extended writing.

Assessment and progression

Students are assessed on comprehension, inference, analysis of language and structure, comparison, contextual understanding and the accuracy and effectiveness of their own writing. Spoken language and discussion support the written curriculum.

Where this can lead

English is a foundation for almost every post-16 route and directly supports careers in law, journalism, media, publishing, teaching, marketing, communications and the creative industries.
