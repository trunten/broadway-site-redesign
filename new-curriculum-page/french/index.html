Why study French?

Learning French develops communication, cultural understanding, memory and confidence while opening a window onto the wider Francophone world.

Curriculum at Broadway

Broadway is a multilingual community and languages are treated as both a practical skill and a way of understanding other cultures. French is taught across Years 7–9, with students developing listening, speaking, reading and writing alongside increasingly accurate grammar and pronunciation.

Year 7

Students establish phonics and pronunciation, common verbs, time and core language patterns through topics such as classroom language, likes and dislikes, personal description, school subjects and routines.

Year 8

Students revisit and extend core grammar, including -er verbs and past and future time frames. Francophone culture becomes more prominent and language is used in increasingly everyday and authentic contexts.

Year 9

Students consolidate the three main time frames, broaden vocabulary and move towards longer, more independent speaking and writing. They decide whether to continue French to GCSE.

Key Stage 4

GCSE French develops confident communication across listening, speaking, reading and writing. Broadway may also support students to take a GCSE in a home language where an examination route is available.

Assessment and progression

Frequent retrieval, vocabulary checks and short assessments build fluency. Students are assessed across listening, speaking, reading and writing, with grammar and accurate pronunciation developed throughout.

Where this can lead

Languages support careers in business, travel, diplomacy, law, international development, interpreting, translation, tourism and any role working across cultures or borders.
