Why study Religious Education?

Religious Education explores faith, belief, ethics and contemporary moral questions while developing knowledge, empathy, respect and well-reasoned argument.

Curriculum at Broadway

The curriculum links beliefs, practices and value systems across faiths and worldviews. Students are encouraged to ask searching questions, understand disagreement respectfully and make informed responses to issues in modern Britain.

Year 7

Students begin with the six major world faiths through a thematic study of different conceptions of God, building basic religious literacy and extended-writing skills.

Years 8–9

Knowledge of beliefs and practices broadens into increasingly demanding philosophical, ethical and contemporary questions. Retrieval practice and extended writing develop both knowledge and evaluation.

GCSE

Students follow Edexcel Religious Studies Specification B. The course examines religion and ethics through Christianity and religion, peace and conflict through Islam, with a strong emphasis on constructing balanced written arguments.

Co-curricular

Opportunities have included visits to places of worship and cathedrals, inter-faith and conflict-resolution work, humanities visits to Island Pool and lectures or masterclasses on philosophy of religion.

Assessment and progression

Students are assessed through knowledge and understanding (AO1) and analysis/evaluation (AO2). GCSE is assessed through external written examinations.

Where this can lead

RE develops skills useful in law, politics, teaching, journalism, community work, theology, philosophy, public service and any field involving ethics and diverse communities.
