Why study Computing & ICT?

Computing develops digital knowledge, logical thinking, problem-solving and the confidence to use technology effectively and responsibly.

Curriculum at Broadway

The curriculum moves beyond simply using computers. Students learn how digital systems work, how information is represented and transmitted, and how to design solutions through programming.

Year 7

Students begin with clear messaging in digital media, then explore networks and how information travels across them. Programming starts in Scratch, building confidence with sequence, selection and iteration.

Years 8–9

Students move into the layers of computing systems, binary and data representation, text-based programming with Python, networks, algorithms and the social, ethical and environmental impact of technology.

Key Stage 4

Students develop more formal computational thinking: decomposition, algorithms, programming, data representation, hardware, operating systems, networks, cyber security and the wider implications of digital technology.

Assessment and progression

Practical programming and problem-solving sit alongside written knowledge. Students are expected to explain algorithms, trace and debug code, understand systems and apply computing concepts to unfamiliar scenarios.

Where this can lead

Computing supports routes into software development, cyber security, data, networks, engineering, digital design and almost every modern technical profession.
