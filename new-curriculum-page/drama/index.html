Drama at Broadway

Drama develops performance, collaboration, creativity and confidence while helping students understand theatre, stories and the world from different perspectives.

Curriculum at Broadway

Broadway’s Drama curriculum is deliberately practical and ambitious. Students learn acting and devising skills, theatre conventions and performance vocabulary while also developing leadership, self-esteem and the ability to work productively with others.

Year 7

Students learn core physical, vocal and performance skills, devising and group leadership. Silent movie and superhero work develops physicality, stock characters, story structure and communicating meaning to an audience.

Year 8

Students deepen their use of conventions through work including Harriet Tubman, a missing-person devising project, improvisation linked to Island Pool, World War II and Macbeth.

Year 9

The focus shifts towards Key Stage 4: conflict and stimulus-based devising, script work, audition technique, monologues and duologues, theatre styles and practitioners.

Years 10–11

Students follow the Performing Arts drama pathway, reproducing existing work, creating and refining original work and responding to an industry-style commission.

Assessment and progression

At Key Stage 3, practical work is assessed through “Head, Hands and Heart”: decision-making and thinking; physical/vocal performance; and engagement, teamwork and rehearsal habits. Key Stage 4 combines practical and written evidence.

Where this can lead

Drama supports progression into acting, directing, stage management, technical theatre, media, teaching and any career where communication, presentation and teamwork matter.
